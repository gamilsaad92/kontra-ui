This is **substantially better**. The Overview now feels like one system rather than several dashboard concepts competing with each other. But the screenshots reveal **one real logic bug and two smaller issues** I would fix before moving on.

### The real bug: “Invite Deal Coordinator”

This is incorrect.

The Overview now says:

> **Invite Deal Coordinator**
> Needed to complete Identity & Parties

But the People tab correctly shows:

> **Deal Coordinator**
> Workspace coordinator · Active
> **Owner**

So the action-generation logic is still independently deciding that the coordinator is missing. That means you fixed the **People rendering**, but not the underlying semantic participant resolution used by Next Actions.

This is important because it shows the same coordinator problem still exists deeper in the application.

The action engine should understand:

**Workspace owner/manager = coordinator satisfied.**

It should never generate:

> Invite Deal Coordinator

for the person currently operating the workspace.

Instead, based on this workspace, that slot should probably become:

> **Invite Buyer Representative**
> Needed to complete Identity & Parties
> `Open People`

because the Buyer Representative is genuinely not invited.

### The readiness wording is now much better

This is a good change:

> **8% Needs Attention**
> Completeness and confirmation of the structured transaction record.
> **0 of 30 required fields confirmed**

That is far more understandable than “0 of 8 readiness areas.”

There is one thing I would verify technically: **how can readiness be 8% while 0 of 30 required fields are confirmed?**

That isn't necessarily wrong. For example, readiness could include extracted/proposed/unconfirmed information, documents, or other completeness signals. But if the description says the percentage measures “completeness and confirmation of the structured transaction record,” users will naturally expect:

**0 / 30 confirmed ≈ 0%**

If the 8% comes from something else, make it explicit. Otherwise I'd make readiness primarily field-based so the percentage and denominator tell the same story.

### Next Actions is finally doing the right job

This is a major improvement:

> Upload Letter of Intent
> Upload Purchase Agreement
> Invite Deal Coordinator
> View all 19 actions

The structure is right. I especially like that it gives the responsible role and a contextual action button without recreating the entire Documents tab.

I would **keep this design**.

The only UX adjustment I'd make is the wording of document actions. The card says **“Upload Letter of Intent”**, but the button says **“Request.”** Those describe different actions.

If the responsible party is Buyer Representative, and the coordinator doesn't possess the document, use:

> **Request Letter of Intent**
> Required · Buyer Representative
> `Request`

If the coordinator is responsible for uploading it:

> **Upload Letter of Intent**
> Required · Deal Coordinator
> `Upload`

That small distinction makes Kontra feel much more operationally intelligent.
