Based on everything you've shared over the past few weeks, I'd separate **Kontra today** from **Kontra's long-term vision** because they're at different stages.

### Kontra today (AI transaction workspace)

I think there is a **plausible market**, but I would not yet say product-market fit has been demonstrated.

Here's why:

**What is strong:**

* The problem is real. Private transactions are still coordinated through email, shared drives, spreadsheets, and ad hoc document requests.
* Your move from a CRE-specific product to a configurable transaction workspace significantly expands the addressable market.
* Workflow Packs make the platform adaptable without rebuilding the product for each industry.
* AI document analysis, role-based access, audit trails, and structured workflows create a coherent product rather than a collection of AI features.

**What's still unproven:**

* Whether people are willing to change how they currently run transactions.
* Which customer segment adopts first (business brokers, M&A advisors, attorneys, lenders, franchise brokers, etc.).
* Whether users find the product valuable enough to pay consistently and recommend it.

Until real customers repeatedly choose Kontra over existing workflows, it's more accurate to say you have **a strong product hypothesis** than confirmed product-market fit.

---

## The tokenization vision

This is where I think the strategy becomes more interesting.

If tokenization becomes mainstream over the next decade, every real-world asset still has to go through preparation:

* due diligence
* document collection
* ownership verification
* legal review
* audit trail
* approvals
* closing documentation

Those steps exist whether the final ownership is represented by paper, a database entry, or a blockchain token.

That's why your idea of Kontra being the **transaction operating system before tokenization** is strategically coherent. It doesn't depend on tokenization succeeding tomorrow; it provides value today while producing structured data that could support tokenization later.

However, this vision depends on a major assumption:

> The market eventually values standardized, digital transaction records that can flow into tokenized ownership systems.

That assumption is plausible, but it won't be proven for years.

---

## Where I think Kontra is today

If I had to place it on a maturity scale:

* **Vision:** 9/10
* **Product quality:** 8/10 (from what you've shown)
* **Product-market fit:** 3–4/10
* **Market opportunity:** 8–9/10
* **Execution risk:** High

The gap isn't the idea—it's validation.

---

## What would convince me you've reached product-market fit?

I'd look for signals like:

* Multiple customers using Kontra for real transactions from start to finish.
* Customers returning for additional transactions without being prompted.
* Referrals from existing users.
* Users saying they would be genuinely disappointed if Kontra disappeared.
* A clear customer segment emerging as the primary buyer.

Once those signals appear consistently, I'd be much more confident calling it product-market fit.

So my assessment is:

* **The market problem is real.**
* **The product direction is coherent.**
* **The tokenization vision is strategically aligned with the product.**
* **Product-market fit has not yet been demonstrated—it has to be earned through real customer adoption rather than inferred from the product itself.**
