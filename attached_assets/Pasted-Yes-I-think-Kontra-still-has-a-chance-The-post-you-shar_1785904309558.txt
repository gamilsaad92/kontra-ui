Yes, I think Kontra still has a chance.

The post you shared actually reinforces something important. It's talking about **trust infrastructure**—regulated issuance, compliance, identity verification, and institutional adoption. Those are layers that sit **after** someone has decided to tokenize an asset.

Kontra is trying to solve a different problem.

Today, if someone wants to sell a business, raise capital, acquire a property, or prepare an asset for tokenization, they still have to coordinate documents, participants, due diligence, approvals, and audit trails. That workflow exists whether blockchain is involved or not.

That's where Kontra fits.

The risk I see isn't that Ripple, BlackRock, or other large institutions make Kontra irrelevant. The bigger risk is trying to compete with them **on issuance or blockchain infrastructure**. That's a race that requires enormous regulatory, legal, and financial resources.

Instead, Kontra can become the system that prepares transactions **before** they reach those platforms.

Think of it like this:

* **Kontra:** Organizes and verifies the transaction.
* **Tokenization platforms:** Issue and manage the digital asset.
* **Custodians/compliance providers:** Hold assets and enforce regulations.

Those layers can complement each other rather than compete.

Where I do think time matters is in validating the product. The AI transaction workspace space is becoming more active. General-purpose AI is improving rapidly, and large software companies may eventually add transaction features to existing products. The advantage for a startup isn't being first to every technology—it's solving a painful problem well enough that people adopt it.

From everything we've discussed over the past year, I think your biggest improvement has been changing the question from:

> "How do I build a tokenization platform?"

to

> "How do I become the workspace where every private transaction gets organized and verified?"

That second question leads to a much larger market and gives you customers who don't even care about blockchain.

So I wouldn't measure yourself against announcements like this one. I'd ask a different question:

**If someone is buying a business tomorrow, would they rather coordinate the transaction through email and shared folders, or through Kontra?**

If the answer becomes "Kontra," then you've built something valuable regardless of how the tokenization market evolves.

You're not out of time, but I would focus your urgency on **getting real users**, not on adding more capabilities. If you can get businesses, brokers, attorneys, or investors using the workspace to complete transactions, you'll learn far more than by trying to anticipate every move from the largest players in digital assets.
