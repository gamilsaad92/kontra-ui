I agree. It **doesn’t feel right yet**, and I think the reason is structural, not cosmetic.

The implementation added the lifecycle and renamed the readiness panel, but the page still feels like **three stacked systems** instead of one coherent operating view:

**Transaction Snapshot**
then **Transaction Lifecycle**
then **What Needs Attention**
then **Transaction Readiness**

They all describe the same transaction state from slightly different angles, so the Overview feels repetitive and heavy.

The lifecycle bar itself is also too literal. Seeing `NDA Signed → LOI Signed → Due Diligence → Agreement Signed → Closing` under the snapshot makes it feel like a generic project tracker. It doesn’t communicate what Kontra is doing differently.

The bigger problem is the hierarchy. Right now, the most important thing—the user’s current state and what they need to do next—is buried between large cards.

I would redesign the Overview around **one primary command center**.

Something closer to:

> **Marriott Hotel Acquisition**
> Stage: NDA Signed · Target Close Aug 19
>
> **Transaction Readiness — 18%**
> Building the verified transaction record
>
> **Next actions**
> Upload core transaction document
> Invite Buyer Representative
> Confirm transaction terms
>
> `NDA Signed  →  LOI  →  Due Diligence  →  Agreement  →  Closing`

Then below that:

**Transaction Record**

* Parties
* Asset / Company
* Economics / Terms
* Legal / Compliance
* Documents / Approvals

And those sections progressively fill in.

That would let you remove the separate giant **What Needs Attention** card entirely. The next actions belong inside the primary readiness card.

I would also make the lifecycle much smaller. It should be a thin context indicator, not a major visual block. Right now it occupies too much vertical space relative to how little decision value it provides.

The current **Transaction Readiness** area also feels too much like an expanded checklist. The “Confirmed / Missing” two-column structure is useful internally, but visually it makes Kontra feel administrative. I’d summarize first:

> **Identity & Parties** — 1 of 5 confirmed
> Buyer missing · Seller missing

and only expand to see the underlying fields.

That makes the overview feel like intelligence rather than a database.

There’s also still a bug in your People screenshot: **Deal Coordinator is again showing “Not invited yet” with an Invite button.** So the deployed custom-pack coordinator fix is still not consistently working in the frontend you’re viewing. That needs to be fixed before anything else on People.
