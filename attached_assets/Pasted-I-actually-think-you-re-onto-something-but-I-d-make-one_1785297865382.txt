I actually think you're onto something, but I'd make one distinction:

**Don't change Kontra from a deal room into an "open room." Change it into a transaction workspace.**

That sounds subtle, but it's a much bigger market.

Right now, Kontra is essentially saying:

> "We help you complete a CRE acquisition or business acquisition."

Instead, it could say:

> "If multiple parties need to collaborate around an important transaction, Kontra is the workspace."

That still includes:

* Commercial real estate acquisitions
* Business acquisitions
* Capital raises
* Joint ventures
* Private equity diligence
* Asset sales
* Loan originations
* Franchise purchases
* M&A
* Family office investments
* Infrastructure projects
* Energy projects
* Any high-value transaction involving documents, tasks, approvals, and multiple parties

The important part is that **the architecture barely changes.**

Every transaction still has:

* Participants
* Documents
* AI analysis
* Tasks
* Questions
* Approvals
* Audit trail
* Closing package

The workflow pack changes—not the platform.

---

### This actually helps tokenization

One thing we've talked about before is that **tokenization should be downstream**, not the product itself.

If Kontra becomes the operating system for **all private asset transactions**, suddenly you're collecting structured data on far more assets than just CRE.

Imagine five years from now.

Someone finishes:

* buying a business
* purchasing a hotel
* acquiring a warehouse
* investing in a private company
* financing a solar farm

Every one of those leaves Kontra with:

* verified ownership
* audit trail
* structured documents
* participant identities
* closing package

Those are exactly the ingredients needed to make an asset tokenization-ready later.

So instead of saying:

> "Kontra tokenizes real estate."

You eventually say:

> "Any asset that passes through Kontra can become digitally transferable."

That's a much bigger vision.

---

### I wouldn't call it an "Open Deal Room"

Personally, I think "Open Deal Room" sounds like:

* Dropbox
* Google Drive
* a generic file-sharing product

It loses the sense that Kontra is coordinating a **transaction**.

I'd rather position it as:

> **Kontra is the operating system for private asset transactions.**

Then underneath:

* CRE Acquisition
* Business Acquisition
* Fundraising
* Debt Financing
* Asset Sale
* Joint Venture
* Custom Workflow

Notice that **Custom Workflow** is the escape hatch. If a customer has a transaction you didn't anticipate, they can start with a blank template instead of being forced into CRE or Business Acquisition.

---

### I think this is the evolution

Originally:

> AI Deal Room for Commercial Real Estate

↓

Current:

> AI Transaction Workspace for CRE & Business Acquisitions

↓

What I think becomes strongest:

> **AI Transaction Workspace for Private Asset Transactions**

That keeps the product focused—you still solve a specific problem: coordinating complex transactions—but it dramatically expands your addressable market without abandoning the foundation you've already built. It also fits the long-term roadmap you've been aiming for, where completed transactions produce structured, auditable data that can later support tokenization rather than leading with blockchain.
