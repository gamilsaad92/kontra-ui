Yes — this iteration fixed the main contradiction I flagged. The **coordinator problem is now resolved across the visible surfaces**, and the Overview is much closer to the architecture we were aiming for.

There are still a few things I would correct before calling this finished.

### What is now right

The cross-screen logic is finally lining up:

* **Overview:** `Invite Buyer` rather than `Invite Deal Coordinator` ✅
* **People:** Deal Coordinator = `Workspace coordinator · Active · Owner` ✅
* **People denominator:** `0 of 4 participants invited` excludes the coordinator ✅
* **Documents:** responsibility is assigned to Buyer/Seller rather than coordinator incorrectly appearing everywhere ✅
* **Next Actions:** `Request Letter of Intent` → `Request` now matches the actual operation ✅
* **Readiness:** explicitly says `0 of 30 required fields confirmed` rather than inventing an area-completion concept ✅

That is a meaningful architectural improvement.

## But I see 4 remaining issues

### 1. The 8% is still semantically confusing

They changed the description to:

> Operational readiness across documents, workspace facts, activity, and compliance signals.

That technically explains why **8% can coexist with 0/30 confirmed fields**.

But I don't think this is the right direction.

We specifically separated:

**Lifecycle** = where the transaction is.

**Transaction Readiness** = how complete/confirmed the transaction record is.

Now they've quietly broadened Transaction Readiness into an **operational health score** again.

That risks recreating the conceptual problem we just removed.

I'd rather have:

> **TRANSACTION READINESS**
> **0%**
> Completeness and confirmation of the structured transaction record.
> 0 of 30 required fields confirmed

Documents, activity, compliance, etc. can influence **Next Actions**, warnings, and perhaps later a separate transaction-health concept. They shouldn't make an unconfirmed Transaction Record look 8% verified.

This matters even more once AI starts extracting information. You eventually want something like:

**Proposed → Confirmed**

rather than treating the existence of a document as equivalent to verified transaction truth.

---

### 2. The Transaction Record still contains duplicate/generic party fields

Expand **Identity & Parties** and look at the missing list:

> Buyer / primary party
> Seller / counterparty
> Primary party
> Secondary party
> +2 more

That is not good.

For a Marriott acquisition pack, users shouldn't simultaneously be asked for:

* Buyer
* Seller
* Primary party
* Secondary party

Those are overlapping concepts.

The generic schema appears to be leaking through underneath the pack-specific schema.

For this transaction it should probably be something like:

> Buyer
> Seller
> Buyer Representative
> Seller Representative
> Legal Advisor
> Financial Advisor

And for another transaction type, the pack maps its own semantics.

This is more than cosmetic. Otherwise Kontra can eventually say:

> Buyer confirmed

while simultaneously saying:

> Primary party missing

even though they're conceptually the same entity.

That would undermine the entire idea of the Transaction Record.

---

### 3. Documents and Next Actions need one more responsibility check

The current Documents screen assigns:

> Letter of Intent → Buyer
> Purchase Agreement → Buyer
> NDA → Buyer
> Due Diligence Report → Buyer

That's possible, but I would verify these are intentionally generated by the pack rather than arbitrary defaults.

More importantly, the Overview says:

> **Request Purchase Agreement**
> Required · Buyer

That's internally consistent with Documents, which is good.

But Kontra's rule should be:

**document responsibility comes from the workflow pack/document item → Next Actions consumes it.**

Next Actions should never independently infer who owes a document.

That will prevent these systems from drifting again.

---

### 4. Lifecycle truncation shouldn't happen at this desktop width

This:

> Preliminary Te...
> Final Negotiati...

looks unfinished.

You have plenty of horizontal room.

At this viewport, render:

**NDA Signing → Preliminary Terms → Due Diligence → Final Negotiation → Closing**

without truncation.

On narrower screens, shortening can happen responsively. But a 1440-ish desktop command center shouldn't abbreviate five stage names.
